# DrumPanel STEM check

Verdict: every claim the bench set makes sits within known science once five details found by the independent review are fixed, and nothing fails, so `stem_check` is **pass**. The review refuted the claim that one bending roll reading holds for a batch of drums and found four more details that failed: one drain stand could not purge drums fast enough, a methane-calibrated LEL sensor can under-read heavy vapour, the 8 mm guard slots sat on the edge of ISO 13857 Table 4, and fire-resistant hydraulic fluids do not settle out of purge water. Each was fixed in the design under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 (DMP-DEC-001, fixes F1 to F5) and its row closed. Of the 28 claims for option A, the current design, 23 are within known science and 5 are plausible and closed by the calculation note DMP-CAL-001 on paper (the two hardest hand forces, 136 N and 131 N against 150 N, the force limit itself, the single driven disc's traction and the cut zone temperature); each rests on a screening value that the TRL 4 trial measures. The 8 claims for option B, the lever shear and stretch leveller, are first-order screens and within known science. Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which takes its option A numbers from `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py`.

> **Safety:** The STEM check shows that the physics and chemistry hold on paper. It does not show that a given drum is free of vapour: the vapour check on every drum, every day, with a detector calibrated for heavy hydrocarbon vapour, is the safeguard (S4), and drums that held fuel, solvent, chemicals, fire-resistant hydraulic fluids or unknown contents are never processed.

Verdicts: **Within known science**, **Plausible, to confirm at TRL 3**, **Fails, fix proposed**, **Fails**. A plausible row is closed when DMP-CAL-001 gives its number on paper; the coefficient it rests on is measured at TRL 4. Rows S4, S10, S16, S19 and S25 failed as first written and are closed by fixes F1 to F5.

Two notes on what the table does not say:

- S4 rests on the acceptance rule (only drums labelled for non-volatile oils with flash points above 60 °C are taken in) and on the reading at both bungs. The 60 °C limit is the upper bound of GHS flammable liquid category 3; liquids with flash points from 60 to 93 °C (GHS category 4) are still accepted by that rule. The vapour check catches any vapour they leave, so the claim holds; whether to tighten the rule to 93 °C is open decision 2 for Amish in DMP-DEC-001, not changed here.
- S11 was worded in DMP-CAL-001 v0.2 as strain "far past what the sheet can take without tearing or buckling". Low-carbon drum steel stretches well past 8.5 % before it tears; what the slip roll cannot do is put that strain in evenly, so a rolled hoop leaves the panel buckled. The design is unchanged (the hoops are cut out); the wording is corrected in DMP-CAL-001 v0.3, and the row is a design rationale.

## Fields not relevant

- Physics, electromagnetism and circuits, optics and radiation, nuclear and ionising radiation: the bench set has no electrical, optical or radioactive parts; the gas detector is a bought instrument used to its manual.
- Chemistry, electrochemistry and batteries: the only battery is in the bought gas detector.
- Biology, microbiology, ecology and agronomy, plant and animal biology, epidemiology: no living material is processed; purge water is settled, skimmed and reused (S19).
- Engineering and technology, power and link budgets, control, sensing resolution: no power or control system; the detector's resolution is the bought instrument's (its alarm is set at 5 % LEL).

## Concept options

- **Option A, bench set: rotary shears and slip roll** (the current design; recommended, proposed, awaiting Amish): rows S1 to S28, within known science or plausible and closed by DMP-CAL-001, with fixes F1 to F5. Pass.
- **Option B, lever shear and stretch leveller**: rows B1 to B8, within known science at first order. A 1,050 mm lever through a link gives about 22 to 1 at the cut, so one person shears 1.5 mm steel at about 122 N (B1); a 15 t hand-pumped cylinder stretches the widest, thickest panel 2 % past yield at 92.7 kN (B2), which wipes out the curl (B3); the wedge grips are set at 7 degrees so the serrated face out-grips the greased back (B6, corrected after the review). Its costs are elsewhere: each panel loses about 86 mm of length to the grips (B7, short of the 1,800 mm of PR-012) and the many short lever strokes make it slower, about 1.65 drums an hour (B8). Pass at first order; a calculation note would be written only if Amish chose it.
