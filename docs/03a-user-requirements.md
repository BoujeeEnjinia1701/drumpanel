# DrumPanel user requirements

What the people who open drums, build the tools and buy the sheet need, written from their side. Fifteen needs, eight of them High. They are drawn from the users and stakeholders tables of DMP-PRB-001 v0.4 and its constraints; every need traces to product requirements in DMP-PRS-001 (matrix: DMP-RTM-001), and the same needs scored the concept options (DMP-OPT-001). This is a desk draft: a cooperative of drum artists and a vocational welding school review it before TRL 4 (co-design candidates in DMP-PRB-001; nothing agreed). Method: STANDARDS section 22.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Safety | No fire, no smoke | Drums are opened without burning anything, so there is no smoke from paint and residue in the yard or the neighbourhood | High | Metal artists and apprentices; neighbours (PRB Table 1, Table 2) |
| UR-002 | Safety | Know the drum is safe | Before anyone cuts, we know the drum holds no flammable vapour, from a simple check we trust | High | Apprentices; cooperative; vocational school (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-003 | Performance | Heads off, cold and quick | Both heads come off a drum cold, in a few minutes, as flat discs we can use | Medium | Metal artists and apprentices |
| UR-004 | Performance | Clean, straight cuts | The side opens along a straight line and the cut edges are clean, so the sheet is ready for art | Medium | Metal artists |
| UR-005 | Performance | Flat sheet | The sheet comes off flat enough to mark out and cut designs without hours of hammering | High | Metal artists |
| UR-006 | Usability | Hand power that does not wear us out | The tools work by hand, with no electricity, and an apprentice can work them all day | High | Apprentices; workshops without reliable power (PRB operating environment) |
| UR-007 | Performance | Enough sheet for a workshop cluster | Two people prepare enough drums in a working day to keep a cluster of workshops in sheet | Medium | Artisan cooperative |
| UR-008 | Maintenance | Made and mended locally | A town fabricator with a welder and a lathe can build and repair the set from drawings and commodity parts | High | Local fabricators (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-009 | Cost | Affordable for a cooperative | A cooperative or a fair trade partner can fund one shared set | Medium | Artisan cooperative; fair trade buyers |
| UR-010 | Safety | No trapped fingers | Nobody's fingers can reach a roll, disc, gear or chain while the tools are worked | High | Apprentices; cooperative |
| UR-011 | Safety | No heavy lifts | Nobody lifts more than they safely can, and nobody tips a drum full of water | Medium | Apprentices |
| UR-012 | Performance | Useful sheet from every drum | Every drum gives sheets of a known, useful size and the heads as discs | High | Metal artists; fair trade buyers |
| UR-013 | Environment and durability | Oil kept out of the ground | Residue and purge water are caught, settled and reused, and the oil goes to a recycler, never onto the ground | Medium | Communities near the yards; drum collectors and recyclers (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-014 | Usability | A method we can follow from pictures | Every step, from accepting a drum to stacking the sheet, can be followed from pictogram sheets in our own language | Medium | Cooperative; vocational school |
| UR-015 | Safety | Only safe drums come in | Drums that held fuel, solvent, chemicals or unknown contents never enter the process | High | Drum collectors and recyclers; cooperative (PRB constraints) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
